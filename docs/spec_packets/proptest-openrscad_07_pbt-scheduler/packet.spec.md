# Tiny independent models for scheduling, claims and declaration aggregation

## Goal

Complete H-DAG, H-CLAIMS and H-REGISTRY through independently oracled scheduler properties, concrete defect regressions and measured Windows routine/extended execution.

## Why

The approved input is `docs/specs/proptest-openrscad-adoption.md` §§5–7, §10, §12 and G-FAMILY. Scheduling mistakes can silently omit a module, accept conflicting writers or select the wrong claim holder. Random acyclic graphs and success-only assertions cannot establish those contracts. Small exhaustive order/path models and typed scope/declaration models can.

Grounding also finds boundaries the tests must preserve: `topological_sort` returns the entire blocked remainder, not just strongly connected nodes; validation has its own private sorter; effective modules/claim observations are supplied to the validator, not filtered by an imaginary enable flag; and incompatible cross-manifest declarations use first-seen warning semantics rather than unconditional permutation invariance.

## Scope

- In: the three assigned charters; tiny valid graphs, cycle-plus-tail witnesses and separately malformed raw models; graph construction and plan assembly observations; reachability/write ordering; effective claim scopes, required capabilities and disabled-candidate removal; exact fill/family exceptions; declaration-only aggregation, the scheduler helper's canonical ordering, manifest rejection and warning conditions; the non-policy ADR-0018/doc clarification distinguishing the shipped RegionMapping driver from the scheduler helper and recording warning payloads; deterministic oracle controls, measured profiles, actual Windows execution and focused fixes with named concrete regressions for confirmed defects.
- Out: H-REGIONMAP driver properties or changes to its axis order, new WIT/IR fields or schema versions, changing claim policy, new general raw-graph validators, widening private APIs, runtime support aggregation/dispatch enforcement, SCAD, module algorithms, general guest/E2E randomization and final rollout acceptance. A larger confirmed defect blocks the affected charter rather than weakening/removing it.

## Design notes

### Grounded seams and test homes

- Public `topological_sort(&[ModuleNode]) -> Result<Vec<ModuleId>, Vec<ModuleId>>` (`crates/slicer-scheduler/src/topology.rs`) uses lexical minimum-ready ordering and deduplicates outgoing destinations. `ModuleNode.edges_to: Vec<EdgeTo>` contains `EdgeTo { to, reasons }`, not plain strings (`crates/slicer-scheduler/src/dag.rs`). `EdgeReason::{IrWriteRead { writer_path }, ExplicitRequires}` lives in `crates/slicer-scheduler/src/instrumentation.rs`. `build_intra_stage_dag(StageId, &[&dyn Producer])` builds deterministic nodes/edges; use `LoadedModuleBuilder` rather than inaccessible fields.
- Public `validate_startup_dag(&DagValidationRequest) -> DagValidationReport` drives private claim/write/dependency/cycle passes (`crates/slicer-scheduler/src/validation.rs`). Observe diagnostics by `DagValidationPass`, severity and actual `SchedulerError` fields; do not expose private `compute_reachability` or call it as an oracle. The validator's private sorter is distinct from the public sorter, so cyclic cases must reach both public ordering and `CycleDetection`.
- `DagValidationRequest` contains `modules`, `stage_dags`, `host_ir_schema_version`, `host_version`, `claim_holders`, `access_audits`; it has no module-disable field. Production constructs global observations in the validation request in `crates/slicer-runtime/src/run.rs`. Synthetic `ConflictScope::Region` tests exercise the public validator contract, **not proof of live region-scoped enforcement**. Support ownership/uniqueness at the host aggregation seam remains outside this packet.
- `build_execution_plan` / `ExecutionPlanRequest` / `ExecutionModuleBinding` / `SortedStageModules` (`crates/slicer-scheduler/src/execution_plan.rs`) accept already sorted stages and bound modules. They reject `MissingModuleBinding`, `DuplicateModuleBinding`, `StageMismatch` and `UndeclaredConfigKey`; they are not a second graph sorter. Use real `bind_module_config_view` for valid empty/declared config fixtures.
- `aggregate_region_splits` and `canonical_variant_chain_order` (`crates/slicer-scheduler/src/region_split.rs`) operate on loaded declarations; `build_execution_plan` (`crates/slicer-scheduler/src/execution_plan.rs`) actually calls the aggregator and stores its output in `ExecutionPlan.aggregated_region_split`. The ordering helper has no production caller in the current Rust tree; a direct helper assertion is not evidence about RegionMapping. Raw TOML rejection belongs to `load_module_from_paths` (`crates/slicer-scheduler/src/manifest.rs`). The builder bypasses ingestion validation and must not be used to prove invalid manifests reject.
- `commit_region_mapping_builtin` (`crates/slicer-runtime/src/builtins/region_mapping_producer.rs`) passes `plan.aggregated_region_split` to `execute_region_mapping_inner` (`crates/slicer-core/src/algos/region_mapping.rs`). The latter supplies the map's **name-ordered keys**, not the scheduler helper's priority/name sequence, to `enumerate_canonical_chains` (`crates/slicer-ir/src/region_split_registry.rs`), which preserves the supplied axis order. This is source-inspected driver behavior, not an H-REGIONMAP test result or a policy decision to prefer name order.
- Add **new** standalone targets/files `property_scheduler` / `crates/slicer-scheduler/tests/property_scheduler.rs` and `property_scheduler_contracts` / `crates/slicer-scheduler/tests/property_scheduler_contracts.rs`, both explicitly `required-features = ["property_testing"]`. Target-private helpers may be explicitly registered beneath these roots. No shared public SDK export is needed. Preserve the scheduler's normal-dependency separation from runtime/wasmtime; a dev-only SDK edge is not a production edge.

### Charter H-DAG: exhaustive orders and independent blocked remainders

Generate `SmallGraph` with checked `3 <= max_nodes <= 7`; permit empty/singleton DAG cases and preserve a two-node cycle plus downstream acyclic tail in `CycleWithTail`. Map each u16 node to a unique fixed-width lexical module string (`com.pbt.n` plus five decimal digits), independently of input Vec order. Convert edges to `EdgeTo` with genuine reasons; bounded duplicate destinations/reason permutations are a separate public-sorter robustness mode. Valid DAG-builder input has unique IDs/endpoints, unique declared paths and no fabricated self-requires.

For DAGs enumerate **all** index permutations, retain those satisfying every edge's precedence, and select the lexicographically minimum module-ID sequence. This is an independent finite linear-extension definition, not another ready-queue/Kahn implementation. Assert exact returned order, full bijection and precedence. Empty input has the one empty linear extension. Compare after node/edge input permutations; repeated destinations cannot change the public sorter's result. The factorial search is capped by seven nodes; do not quietly raise this bound when extended cases become costly.

For cyclic graphs use #2's nonempty-path `graph_reachability` / `graph_cycle_remainder`: the error set is exactly cycle nodes plus every descendant of a cycle, including the acyclic tail, excluding independent sortable nodes. The public error Vec is lexical. For `validate_startup_dag` compare the `CycleDetection` error's `cycle` **set** to that model, not an invented simple-cycle route or lexical order the private sorter does not promise. Add deterministic self-cycle and disconnected-cycle witnesses independently of the shared cycle-tail strategy. Keep cycles out of the successful-plan domain.

Exercise `build_intra_stage_dag` on bounded loaded-module declarations: expected edge relation is exact same-stage write/read path intersection plus reversed `requires_modules` (required producer before consumer). Assert destination uniqueness, reasons as exact sets and stage exclusion. Use a relation-table model, not the builder to construct expectations. Build a real execution plan from the valid sorted output and bindings; compare compiled module sequence/claims/access masks and `paint_only` semantic propagation to inputs, accounting for the existing host `Layer::PaintRegionAnnotation` insertion. Do not require all returned stages to contain modules or pretend plan assembly validates arbitrary supplied orders.

Malformed `SmallGraph` duplicate-node/unknown-endpoint cases are rejected by #2's independent raw graph oracles and a target-private checked conversion before any production sorter call. There is **no** promised scheduler raw-graph rejection API here. This is generator/adapter negative coverage, not SUT-validator credit. Actual invalid scheduling negatives are cycles, missing dependencies and invalid plan bindings at their real validating APIs.

### Charter H-CLAIMS: typed scope/claim and write models

Build small effective-universe records with unique module IDs, compatible versions and canonical stages from `STAGE_ORDER` (`crates/slicer-scheduler/src/execution_plan.rs`). Generate bounded stage-local DAGs plus explicit dependency relations, read/write path choices and holder observations. Compare only the named passes' exact normalized diagnostics; satisfy all four fill coverage obligations in unrelated cases and assert that setup exists, rather than allowing unrelated errors to make a rejection pass. Separate valid and deliberately failing modes.

Independent models are typed tuples, ordered sets and finite nonempty paths. Never concatenate object/region/layer strings into the oracle's scope key. Generate ordinary UUID-like object IDs and bounded raw string IDs containing `:` in a separate validator-boundary mode: `ConflictScope` stores strings and promises no delimiter grammar. Distinct tuples must not alias, and diagnostics must retain original object/region identity. Shrinks preserve the particular conflict/nonconflict witness and both differing identities.

The reviewed exception table is explicit:

| Surface | Required rule |
|---|---|
| Global ordinary claim | Distinct effective holders conflict pairwise; duplicate observations of the same module do not. `claim:raft-fill` is ordinary here, not one of the four configured fill exceptions. |
| Four configured fill IDs | Exactly `claim:top-fill`, `claim:bottom-fill`, `claim:bridge-fill`, `claim:sparse-fill`: global multiplicity allowed, global absence produces `MissingDependency { module: "fill-role-claim:" + claim, requires: "no module holds " + claim }`; per-region duplicate holders still conflict. Derive the four-role set from the normative catalog and cross-check `FILL_CLAIM_IDS`; do not silently credit raft as configured fill. |
| Family support globals | `support-generator`, `support-planner`, `support-family:traditional`, `support-family:tree` are exempt from global conflict at this API. Do not generalize the validator's finite exception table to all `support-family:*` strings. Live per-region family enforcement is delegated to host aggregation. |
| Region observations | Compare `(object_id, region_id, Option<layer>)` as values, with duplicate modules collapsed. Sparse-fill/support-generator transitions on different layers are allowed; same scope still rejects two holders. |
| Stable claim witnesses | `perimeter-generator`, `seam-placer`, `layer-planner`, `mesh-analyzer` must not change holders across layers for one object (`docs/01_system_architecture.md` § Allowed Claim Transition Matrix). Test same-region transitions plus different-region observations on different layers of the same object; other objects remain independent. Require `ClaimConflict` with the claim, distinct module pair and original object identity; same-region cross-layer conflicts retain the original region and `global_layer_index = None`. Do not infer object-wide stability from only a same-region example. |

The last rule follows the normative object-wide wording, not current string-key implementation details. Source inspection shows `scope_key_without_layer` still includes region and serializes delimiters. Those are explicit investigation/focused-fix surfaces, **not executed failure claims** and not permission to narrow generated IDs or the normative stable-claim domain. Preserve the permitted sparse/support transitions and family-specific retention while fixing confirmed cases. This packet does not resolve ambiguous temporal behavior for every other claim ID: use other ordinary IDs only in global/same-layer collision modes, with no new blanket temporal policy.

For write conflicts, independently determine shared exact paths and path reachability. A valid transform consumes the shared field: a downstream writer must declare that field in its `ir_reads`, and a path from the upstream writer must exist. An arbitrary explicit ordering with no shared-field consumption is not a transform exemption. Family support writers are orderable only when both have one of the API's family-support observations and the shared path is exactly `SupportPlanIR`/`SupportIR` or a dot-descendant; `SupportIRx` and unrelated fields are not exempt. Compare `WriteConflict { field, unordered module pair, stage, orderable: false }`; compare normalized sets, since request-node order can orient a reported pair. No claim exemption blanket-disables write validation.

Model module and capability dependencies by exact effective-universe membership. Model direct/transitive stage legality by `STAGE_ORDER` rank and independent finite paths. Assert `MissingDependency { module, requires }`, `CrossStageDependency` stage fields, and that each `TransitiveStageDependency.path` is a real path ending at a later stage; expected existence comes from the model, not the particular BFS route. Generate an enabled-only universe by removing locally tagged disabled modules **and their holder observations and DAG nodes** before building the production request/plan. A retained consumer requiring the removed module/capability must reject; removing a competing holder eliminates only its conflict. This tests effective-input behavior, not nonexistent config parsing/disable machinery.

Exercise `resolve_held_claims`, `FillHolders`, `module_id_matches_holder`, typed `dedup_same_claim_modules_with_typed_wall_generator` and `validate_support_family_pairing` (`crates/slicer-scheduler/src/{validation,execution_plan}.rs`) using a declarative selector table: configured full/core-short IDs match case-sensitively, community short names do not; only declared configured roles and manifest-declared raft survive. On a controlled domain with one contested ordinary claim per module, startup dedup keeps the lexical candidate per stage, respects typed classic/arachne/vase perimeter selection, retains all four fill candidates and support renderers/family planners; incomplete family pairs warn, not abort. Compare actual kept modules and diagnostics, not only helper return booleans. Do not copy the multi-pass dedup implementation into the oracle or claim arbitrary overlapping multi-claim optimization.

### Charter H-REGISTRY: compatible permutation invariance, explicit conflicting mode

Generate unique module IDs and per-manifest unique semantics with legal `RegionSplitValueType::{Flag, ToolIndex, CustomString}` declarations (`custom_string` in TOML). For **compatible** repeated semantics, a relational group model derives one `(priority, type, sorted distinct declaring_modules)` entry. Expected aggregate is precisely those semantics, with no seeded `material`/`fuzzy_skin`. Its `BTreeMap` keys iterate in semantic-name order; a **direct call** to `canonical_variant_chain_order` returns a separate sequence sorted `(priority, name)`. Module/declaration permutations preserve the map, the helper sequence and normalized tied-priority warning contents. Repeating the same loaded module directly at the aggregation seam cannot duplicate `declaring_modules`; do not pass duplicate module bindings to a plan builder that rejects them.

For deliberately incompatible cross-manifest contracts, test the documented first-seen winner and one conflict warning for each later incompatible declaration; no order-invariance claim applies. Derive the first declaration with a linear positional selection, group compatible later declarers, and retain the conflicting declaration's module, its WASM path and both contracts in warning observations. Different semantics tied at one priority cause one nonfatal warning per tied priority group, with semantic names, declaring module IDs, priority and lexical tiebreaker order, even when the same module declares both semantics. Current aggregate diagnostics use an empty path sentinel for a priority group and **module IDs, not both manifest paths** in that message. Preserve preexisting diagnostics (append, never clear).

ADR-0018 §Decision conflates the name-keyed map and priority/name ordering: its cross-product wording says `BTreeMap` order but misleadingly qualifies name ordering as being within a priority tier. `docs/03_wit_and_manifest.md` and `docs/04_host_scheduler.md` also incorrectly say RegionMapping uses the helper; their tied-warning path wording is stale. Amend ADR-0018 in place — no new ADR, no policy change — with the complete wording below, and reconcile **all** those affected ordering and diagnostic passages in docs/03 and docs/04. Record the driver/helper discrepancy explicitly rather than blessing name order as a new policy or asserting that the helper is integrated. #11 owns the driver audit/property; it is not a prerequisite for #7's helper/aggregation coverage.

> ## Amendment: priority/name helper, current RegionMapping order, and tied-priority payload

> This amendment distinguishes the scheduler's ordering contract from the current RegionMapping implementation and clarifies the diagnostic payload. Where historical descriptions of map iteration, actual cross-product implementation or tie-warning provenance conflict with the clarification below, they are superseded by it. It changes no policy and authorizes no production reordering.
>
> - **Container and scheduler helper.** `aggregate_region_splits` stores entries in a `BTreeMap<String, AggregatedRegionSplitEntry>` keyed and iterated by semantic name. The separate `canonical_variant_chain_order` helper (`crates/slicer-scheduler/src/region_split.rs`) returns the `(priority, name)` sequence specified for canonical priority ordering; raw map iteration is not that sequence in general.
> - **Current RegionMapping driver.** `commit_region_mapping_builtin` (`crates/slicer-runtime/src/builtins/region_mapping_producer.rs`) passes `plan.aggregated_region_split` to `execute_region_mapping_inner` (`crates/slicer-core/src/algos/region_mapping.rs`), which passes the map's name-ordered keys to `enumerate_canonical_chains` (`crates/slicer-ir/src/region_split_registry.rs`). The enumerator preserves the supplied axis order; the scheduler helper has no production caller in the current tree. Thus actual cross-product chain axes are name-ordered even where that differs from the scheduler's `(priority, name)` sequence. This source-observed discrepancy is not a decision to replace priority ordering with name ordering; its driver-level audit and any corrective change require separate coordinated work and fixture/compatibility review.
> - **Tied-priority diagnostic payload.** One warning is emitted per priority shared by two or more distinct semantics, including semantics declared by the same module. Its `level` is `Warning`, `path` is an empty `PathBuf` sentinel and `field` is `Some("region_split")`. The message names the shared priority, tied semantic names, each semantic's declaring module IDs, and the lexical tiebreaker order; it does not name both manifest paths.

## Acceptance criteria

New planned tests use `C`/`P` and log mechanics defined in Verification.

- **AC-1** `H-DAG` under `P` compares successful public-sort output to the exhaustive lexical minimum linear extension, permutation/precedence and duplicate-edge invariance; compares both sorters' cyclic blocked sets to nonempty-path cycle remainder including an acyclic tail. `scheduler_dag_oracle_controls` under `C` rejects FIFO-instead-of-minimum, missing/duplicate output and cycle-only-tail-omission mutations.

- **AC-3** `H-CLAIMS` under `P` agrees with the typed scope/holder model on ordinary/raft collisions, repeated same-holder observations, fill coverage, global fill/family exceptions, same-scope region collision and the declared stable/transition-capable modes. `scheduler_claim_scope_and_exception_controls` under `C` detects delimiter-aliasing, dropped raft conflicts, blanket family exemptions and lost original identity; object-wide cross-layer stability is not reduced to one region.
- **AC-4** H-CLAIMS compares real write/dependency diagnostics to independent paths/membership/stage ranks, exact shared-field consumption and support-root aggregation rules. `scheduler_write_dependency_and_disabled_controls` under `C` rejects unordered writers, ordering-without-consumption, `SupportIRx` exemption, absent/removed module/capability and later-stage dependencies while retaining allowed transforms/family writes; removing a competitor removes its conflict without resurrecting disabled candidates.
- **AC-5** `scheduler_claim_selection_and_pairing_controls` under `C`, plus H-CLAIMS, observes configured role/raft resolution, core-only short names, typed perimeter/vase selection, fill/family candidate retention and incomplete-pair warnings through actual seams. - **AC-6** `H-REGISTRY` under `P` calls actual `aggregate_region_splits` and `canonical_variant_chain_order`: compatible outputs equal the independent group model and derived `(priority, name)` helper sequence after input permutations, with name-ordered raw map keys, no seeds and no duplicate declarers; incompatible inputs preserve first-seen semantics and warnings instead of false permutation equality. `scheduler_registry_oracle_and_warning_controls` under `C` asserts the explicit priority-versus-name, empty/material-only, duplicate-declarer, first-seen-conflict and tied-priority witnesses above, including the `Warning`/empty `path`/`Some("region_split")` payload and preserved sentinel. No authoring run is represented as test evidence.
- **AC-N1** `scheduler_raw_graph_and_plan_rejections` under `C` rejects duplicate/unknown-endpoint raw models in the checked test adapter/oracles without calling a validated sorter, and separately observes real `MissingModuleBinding`, `DuplicateModuleBinding`, `StageMismatch` and `UndeclaredConfigKey` with their offending IDs/key.
