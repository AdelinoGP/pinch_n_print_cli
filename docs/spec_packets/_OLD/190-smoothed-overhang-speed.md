# 190-smoothed-overhang-speed

## Goal

Replace the whole-entity quantized speed lookup in `modules/core-modules/overhang-classifier-default/src/lib.rs` — today the whole-entity maximum `entity.path.points.iter().filter_map(|p| p.overhang_quartile).max()` **merged with the curl-derived quartile** (`let Some(q) = overhang_q.max(curl_q) else …`, measured in `run_finalization`; the rule being replaced is the **merge**, not the overhang term alone) feeding one `EntityMutation::SetSpeedFactor(overhang_speed(q, config) / base)` — with a per-point port of canonical `ExtrusionQualityEstimator::estimate_extrusion_quality`'s `calculate_speed` lambda: build the six-entry `speed_sections` table from the hardcoded `overhang_overlap_levels` `{90, 75, 50, 25, 13, 0}`, **read each point's `Point3WithWidth.overhang_distance_mm` — the signed, `boundary_offset`-normalised distance stamped by the prepass in packet `193-overhang-distance-prepass-carrier`** — linearly interpolate the speed between bracketing sections, take `min(calculate_speed(curr), calculate_speed(next))` clamped to the role's reference speed, and emit the whole entity as one `EntityMutation::SetPointSpeedFactors` (packet 189's carrier) gated on a new `enable_overhang_speed` config key plus canonical's `!on_first_layer()` condition.

**The distance is read, not computed.** The maintainer ruled **option (C)** on this packet's `[BLOCK]` set: the in-module cross-layer wall-distance scan is dissolved, along with the `+0.5 × outer_wall_line_width` proxy compensation and the `OuterWall`-only-vs-all-roles question that went with it. `ADR-0053` is the decision record. The carrier's signedness and `+ boundary_offset` normalisation are defined **once**, in `docs/spec_packets/_OLD/193-overhang-distance-prepass-carrier.md` §Data and Contract Notes §"The signedness contract", and are cited — never restated — here and in packet 191.

## Problem Statement

The overhang-speed parity record names "smoothed-speed mode" as one of its two remaining open sub-items, and re-verifies it twice against source: "no interpolation/blending logic anywhere in `resolve_feedrate` or the classifier (confirmed unimplemented, still a flat quantized lookup)". This packet closes that sub-item.

### Three corrections to the plan's premise, each measured

`docs/specs/deviation-backlog-remediation-plan.md` locates overhang-speed parity in "`crates/slicer-gcode/src/emit.rs` (`resolve_feedrate`)". Grounded:

1. **`resolve_feedrate` holds no lookup table.** It is a per-role `match` selecting a base speed, then `speed_factor.clamp(0.05, 5.0)` times `base_speed * 60.0`, rounded to three decimals. Nothing in it is quantized and nothing in it needs to change for this packet.
2. **The quantization is in this guest module.** `run_finalization` (`modules/core-modules/overhang-classifier-default/src/lib.rs`) takes the **whole-entity maximum** of `overhang_quartile` and emits one `EntityMutation::SetSpeedFactor(overhang_speed(q, config) / base)`. `quartile_for_distance` buckets through `BAND_BOUNDARY_MULTIPLIERS: [f32; 3] = [0.5, 1.0, 1.5]`.
3. **The two overhang-speed parity features are not independent.** Canonical `ExtrusionQualityEstimator::estimate_extrusion_quality` derives `smallest_distance_with_lower_speed` from the `speed_sections` table this packet builds and passes it as `estimate_points_properties`' `min_distance` argument; the returned `extended_points` — including packet 191's inserted vertices — is the list `calculate_speed` is evaluated over. Verified against the checkout including the `if (!found) smallest_distance_with_lower_speed = -1.f;` fallback. Order is fixed: 189 → 190 → 191.

### The correction that actually shapes this packet: PnP has no per-point overhang distance

Canonical interpolates over a **continuous** per-point distance to the previous layer's boundary. PnP's only per-point overhang signal is `Point3WithWidth.overhang_quartile: Option<u8>` — a four-bucket quantization of that very distance, produced by `annotate_overhangs` (`crates/slicer-core/src/algos/overhang_annotation.rs`) as polygon bands and stamped onto points downstream. **The distance cannot be recovered from the bucket.** Interpolating over quartile-derived pseudo-distances would produce the same step function the module already produces and would be smoothing in name only.

**How the distance is obtained is the question the maintainer ruled on, and the answer is option (C).** This packet **reads** it. The continuous distance is stamped per point by the prepass in packet **`193-overhang-distance-prepass-carrier`**, onto `Point3WithWidth.overhang_distance_mm`, beside the existing `overhang_quartile`. `ADR-0053` is the decision record.

**The design this replaces, stated so a reader of the earlier revision does not reinstate it.** The original draft computed the distance **in-module**, on the reasoning that canonical computes it at G-code time too: `ExtrusionQualityEstimator` builds its distancer from the previous layer's geometry rather than in a prepass, and the module already walks `layers: &[LayerCollectionView]` in Z order with a previous-layer proximity scan for the curled-edge feature. The flaw was not the tier but the **geometry**: canonical measures against `unscaled_prev_layer`, a distancer over the previous layer's **slice boundary**, and a `PostPass::LayerFinalization` guest sees extrusion entities, not slices. The in-module port therefore had to measure against the previous layer's `OuterWall` **centerline** — roughly half an outer-wall width inside the true boundary, biasing every section threshold in the same direction — and then hand-compensate that bias. ADR-0031's Context gives the reason classification moved off walls in the first place: "Walls are merely an inset-by-`line_width/2` proxy for the true cross-section." Compensating a proxy the repo had already rejected outright is what `[BLOCK-2]` was about.

**Under option (C) there is no proxy, no bias, no compensation and no residual row.** Packet 193 measures against the real slice boundary in the prepass, where that geometry is available, and delivers exactly what canonical measures. The `DEV-###` row the option-(B) design owed is **not** filed; packet 193 files its own narrower row for the per-point-vs-per-path `boundary_offset` resolution.

**The carrier's contract is defined once, upstream, and cited here.** `overhang_distance_mm` is **signed** and already **`+ boundary_offset`-normalised** — the same quantity canonical writes into `ExtendedPoint::distance` as `distance_from_lines_extra<SIGNED_DISTANCE>(…) + boundary_offset` under its `<true, true, true, true>` G-code-path instantiation. The normative definition lives at `docs/spec_packets/_OLD/193-overhang-distance-prepass-carrier.md` §Data and Contract Notes §"The signedness contract"; this packet and packet 191 both cite it and **neither restates it**. An earlier revision of this packet declared its in-module distance as an *unsigned* point-to-segment minimum, which every predicate packet 191 ports would have read wrongly — that disagreement is what the single-definition rule exists to prevent.

### Two canonical details that a plausible port gets wrong, both measured

1. **Section speeds are absolute mm/s and their percentages resolve against `ext_perimeter_speed`, not `original_speed`.** `estimate_extrusion_quality`'s signature binds the two separately — `(…, float ext_perimeter_speed, float original_speed, …)` — and the construction loop reads `speed = speeds.get_at(i).percent ? (ext_perimeter_speed * value / 100.0) : value`. `GCode::_extrude` passes `ref_speed` for the first and `speed` for the second, so the terminal `min(extrusion_speed, original_speed)` compares two mm/s values derived from **two different bases**. PnP collapses them today (both are `base_speed(role, config)`, because a PnP speed factor is by definition a multiplier over that same base), and that collapse is correct now and becomes silently wrong the moment PnP grows canonical's `filament_max_volumetric_speed` or `scarf_joint_speed` floors on `ref_speed`. The port therefore keeps them as **two separate named parameters**, `AC-14` pins the base, and the overhang-speed parity progress paragraph records the collapse. Resolving against the wrong base scales every interpolated speed by the ratio of the two — a defect no absolute-value assertion localises.
2. **The tie handling is a flatten, not a de-duplication.** After the sort (ascending distance, ties **higher speed first**), canonical walks the vector overwriting each equal-distance entry's speed with the earlier one's. There is no `std::unique` and no `erase` in the file: the entry count is preserved at six. For the canonical literal `{90, 75, 50, 25, 13, 0}` the distances come out already ascending, so the sort is a no-op in practice — which is precisely the trap, because "simplifying" the flatten to a dedup looks harmless and changes the section count, and therefore which pair `calculate_speed` brackets. `AC-15` pins it.

A third, load-bearing for packet 191: the `path.width` feeding `distance = path.width * (1.0 - overlap/100.0)` is the **same value** passed as `flow_width` to `estimate_points_properties`. Whatever this packet resolves as `path_width` is therefore what packet 191's `min_spacing = flow_width * 0.25` and `boundary_offset = 0.5 * flow_width` must use.

### The "six-band schedule" exclusion, stated rather than silently omitted

The overhang-speed parity record captures a **user decision of 2026-07-03**: "Six-band schedule reclassified … this is a permanent, intentional, accepted deviation, not an open TODO", pointing at `crates/slicer-core/src/algos/overhang_annotation.rs`'s own doc-comment ("This is a recorded, intentional deviation — not a bug"). That decision is about **`annotate_overhangs`' four concentric quartile bands** at `{0.5, 1.0, 1.5} × line_width`, i.e. the geometry of the prepass classification. It is **untouched** by this packet.

That is a different table from canonical's `speed_sections`, whose distances come from `overhang_overlap_levels` `{90, 75, 50, 25, 13, 0}` inside `GCode::_extrude`. In canonical the two coincide because canonical has no quartiles at all and goes straight from distance to speed. In PnP they are separable, and this packet separates them: `overhang_quartile` keeps its four bands and keeps its job (is this point an overhang at all — `AC-N1`), while the **speed** table is canonical's six-entry one, built from an in-module distance. Nothing about the prepass banding changes, no `BAND_BOUNDARY_MULTIPLIERS` edit is in scope, and the accepted deviation stays accepted. State this explicitly in the overhang-speed parity progress paragraph; do not let a reader infer that the accepted deviation was quietly reopened.

## Architecture Constraints

- **`overhang_quartile` stays the gate; the prepass-stamped distance supplies only the magnitude.** The module's manifest declares `[ir-access] reads = ["LayerCollectionIR", "LayerCollectionIR.overhang_quartile"]`. If the rewrite stopped reading the quartile, that declaration would become false and the DAG's cross-stage reader/writer validation would be asserting a dependency the code no longer has. Keep `p.overhang_quartile.is_some()` as the per-point "is this an overhang" test (`AC-N1`). **Under option (C) the manifest must also gain `LayerCollectionIR.overhang_distance_mm`** — the same truthfulness requirement applied in the other direction; see §Code Change Surface.
- **The distance is read, never computed.** Under the maintainer's option (C) ruling this module performs **no** cross-layer boundary scan. It reads `Point3WithWidth.overhang_distance_mm`, stamped by the prepass in packet **`193-overhang-distance-prepass-carrier`**. That value is **signed** and already **`+ boundary_offset`-normalised**; its single normative definition is `docs/spec_packets/_OLD/193-overhang-distance-prepass-carrier.md` §Data and Contract Notes §"The signedness contract", which this packet and packet 191 both **cite and do not restate**. Consuming it as an unsigned magnitude, or re-adding an offset, is the failure mode that definition exists to prevent.
- **`crates/slicer-core/src/algos/overhang_annotation.rs` is out of bounds, including its `BAND_BOUNDARY_MULTIPLIERS`.** The prepass quartile banding is not touched by this packet. **But do not extend that out-of-bounds status to the module's own private duplicate**: `modules/core-modules/overhang-classifier-default/src/lib.rs` declares its own `const BAND_BOUNDARY_MULTIPLIERS: [f32; 3]` whose **only** reader is `quartile_for_distance`. Deleting `quartile_for_distance` (see §Code Change Surface) orphans that const, `dead_code` fires under `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings`, and Step 5 requires that gate clean — so the module's duplicate must be deleted **in the same step as its reader**. Its doc-comment also carries the cross-crate invariant "Keep these two lists numerically identical"; removing the module copy retires that invariant on the module side, and the deletion comment must say so rather than dropping it silently. Whether this packet may restore canonical's six overlap levels at all is an open scope question — see §Open Questions `[BLOCK-1]`/`[BLOCK-2]`/`[BLOCK-3]`.

## Data and Contract Notes

- **IR/manifest contracts.** `factors.len()` MUST equal the target entity's `path.points.len()`. Packet 189's `apply_to` rejects a mismatch with an `Err`, but `crates/slicer-wasm-host/src/dispatch.rs` swallows that `Err` with `log::warn!` — so in the live WASM path an off-by-one is a **silent no-op**, not a failure. Size the vector from `entity.path.points.len()` directly and never from a filtered subset.
- **Factors are multipliers, not speeds.** `EntitySpeedProfile.factors[i] = per_point_speed_mm_s / base_speed(role)`. `resolve_feedrate` re-applies the role base speed and its `clamp(0.05, 5.0)`; emitting absolute mm/s here would be doubly scaled.
- **`ref_speed` and `original_speed` are two canonically distinct bases that this port deliberately collapses — keep them two separate parameters anyway.** Canonical's `estimate_extrusion_quality(…, float ext_perimeter_speed, float original_speed, …)` resolves every percentage section against `ext_perimeter_speed` and **never** against `original_speed`; `GCode::_extrude` passes `ref_speed` for the first and its own `speed` argument for the second, so the terminal `min(extrusion_speed, original_speed)` compares two mm/s values from different bases. In PnP both currently evaluate to `base_speed(role, config)` — not by coincidence but by definition, because a PnP speed factor *is* a multiplier over that base, so the path's unmutated speed and the role reference speed are the same number. That equality is a property of today's PnP, not of the algorithm: canonical additionally floors `ref_speed` by `filament_max_volumetric_speed / mm3_per_mm` and by `scarf_joint_speed` when sloped, neither of which is available to this module and neither of which is borrowed. **Therefore `build_speed_sections` takes `ref_speed` and `calculate_speed` takes `original_speed` as separate named parameters even though the live caller passes the same value to both**, `AC-14` exercises them with deliberately different values, and the overhang-speed parity progress paragraph records the collapse together with the two unborrowed floors. Merging the parameters would make a future divergence unrepresentable and undetectable.
- **Section speeds are absolute mm/s; the conversion to a factor happens once, at the emission boundary.** `calculate_speed` returns mm/s (canonical rounds mm/s), and only when building the `SetPointSpeedFactors` vector is each value divided by `base_speed(role, config)`. Dividing earlier would make the `round` round a multiplier, which quantises to 0 or 1.
- **`path_width` is shared with packet 191, and under option (C) it is fixed upstream rather than chosen here.** Canonical feeds the same `path.width` both to the section-distance formula and to `estimate_points_properties` as `flow_width`. Packet 193 normalises the carrier with `boundary_offset = 0.5 × width` using **the point's own `width`**, so that is what `path_width` must be here and what packet 191's `min_spacing = flow_width × 0.25` and `boundary_offset` must be there. Resolving a second, independently-derived width would silently decouple the six section thresholds from the distances they threshold. Expose it as a named helper rather than computing it inline at a single call site, so all three packets provably share one number.
- **The signedness contract is cited, never restated.** `docs/spec_packets/_OLD/193-overhang-distance-prepass-carrier.md` §Data and Contract Notes §"The signedness contract" is the single normative definition of `overhang_distance_mm` for this packet and for packet 191. **Do not paraphrase it into this file.** The three artifacts previously disagreed about signedness — this packet declared an *unsigned* point-to-segment minimum while every predicate packet 191 ports reads canonical's *signed* value plus `boundary_offset` — and a paraphrase is how that disagreement returns. If the contract needs to change, it changes in packet 193 and both consumers re-read it.
- **WIT boundary.** None crossed by this packet beyond the variant packet 189 already added. The manifest gains keys; `[ir-access]`, `[claims]` and `[stage]` are unchanged, so no DAG edge moves.
- **Determinism/scheduler constraints.** `[hints] layer-parallel-safe = false` already holds for this module and must stay false — **but note the reason changed under option (C) and the old reason is now wrong.** It is no longer "the per-point distance depends on the previous layer's produced segment list", because there is no such list: the distance arrives stamped. The load-bearing dependency that remains is the **curl** feature's, which keeps the previous layer's curled-height points as local state across the Z-order walk (ADR-0032 records that as deliberate). Getting this reason right matters: a future reader who removes the curl feature might otherwise conclude the hint can flip, when in fact the curl feature is now its *only* justification. `nearest`-style scans must still break ties deterministically (`total_cmp`, as the existing `nearest_reference_point` does) — `DEV-093` records that the pipeline already has a non-determinism problem and this packet must not add to it.

## Locked Assumptions and Invariants

- **A point with `overhang_quartile == None` is not an overhang**, regardless of its measured distance. Locked; `AC-N1` defends it, and the module's `[ir-access]` declaration depends on it.
- **All four `overhang_*_4_speed` at 0 ⇒ zero mutations**, unchanged from today. Locked by the existing early return; `AC-10` defends it. Without it, the `< 0.5` guard would turn every section into 100 % and emit a full-length all-1.0 profile on every wall entity of every layer — a behaviour change disguised as a no-op.
- **`enable_overhang_speed` absent ⇒ `true`.** Matches canonical's `ConfigOptionBoolsNullable{ true }` default and keeps existing configs behaving as they do today.
- **First layer emits no speed mutation.** Canonical `!on_first_layer()`; also the only layer with no previous-layer geometry to measure against.
- **Reversibility:** setting `enable_overhang_speed = false` restores the pre-packet behaviour of emitting nothing; setting the four overhang speeds to 0 does the same. No behaviour lock is introduced that config cannot undo.

## Risks and Tradeoffs

- **~~The `OuterWall`-centerline proxy biases every distance.~~ DISSOLVED BY OPTION (C).** Retained as an audit trail, not as a live risk. The option-(B) design measured from the previous layer's wall centerline, ~half an outer-wall width inside canonical's slice boundary, shifting every point toward the "less overhanging" end of the table; it planned a `DEV-###` row and a `[FWD]` on whether to hand-compensate. Under (C) packet 193 measures against the previous layer's **real slice boundary**, so there is no proxy, no bias, no compensation question and no row. **This is the single largest correctness gain of the ruling** and the reason ADR-0031's "walls are merely an inset-by-`line_width/2` proxy" Context sentence is honoured rather than contested.
- **Cost — materially lower than option (B), and the manifest hint may now be *over*-stated.** Option (C) removes the in-module O(points × prev-layer-segments) boundary scan entirely; what remains is the interpolation plus the pre-existing O(points × prev-layer-points) curl scan. The module's manifest declares `estimated-ms-per-layer = 10`. **Re-read its current value rather than assuming `10`**, measure once on a real slice, and update it if wrong in **either** direction — a hint that over-states cost mis-schedules just as a stale under-statement does. The cost option (C) moves rather than removes is the prepass's own distance computation, which is packet 193's to measure.
- **The mirror can rot silently.** `mirrored_run_finalization` duplicates the module's rule in a crate that cannot depend on the module. Its TRIPWIRE is a comment, not a compiler check; AC-9's structural probe is what converts it into something a preflight can verify.
- **Nine new tests plus four rewritten ones in a single binary** is the largest single review surface. Mitigated by giving each AC exactly one named test; the authoritative list is `requirements.md` §In Scope.
